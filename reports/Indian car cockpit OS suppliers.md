# Android takes India's screens; clusters stay sealed

Indian-market cars are converging on **Android-based center screens**, but almost nothing about their instrument clusters is public. **Mahindra** has the best-documented stacks. Its INGLO EVs (BE 6, XEV 9e, XEV 9S) run an **AAOS-based** infotainment layer on a **Qualcomm Snapdragon SA8295P**, which Mahindra integrates itself. Its ICE flagships (XUV700, Scorpio-N, XUV 7XO) use **Visteon SmartCore** domain controllers on Snapdragon cockpit silicon, with a **BlackBerry QNX Hypervisor** underneath. **Tata** buys most head units from **Harman**, with a handful from Visteon, and distributes apps through the **Harman Ignite Store**, which Harman describes as "AAOS-compliant". **Maruti Suzuki** runs two stacks in parallel: a **Bosch-built, Android-based SmartPlay Pro X** on its newest ICE SUVs, and **Automotive Grade Linux plus Qt** for both the cluster and the infotainment on the e Vitara. Toyota's India rebadges carry Maruti's SmartPlay hardware. For **Hyundai, Kia, JSW MG and Skoda-VW India**, only fragments are public: **Visteon** clusters on early Creta, Seltos and Sonet models, **Visteon and VW do Brasil "Play" head units** on the MQB-A0-IN cars, and **Jio** as MG's services layer. Hyundai Motor Group's AAOS-based **Pleos Connect** is expected in India but not confirmed. No public source names the cluster OS for any current Indian car, and outside Mahindra and the Tata Harrier.ev no source names a head-unit SoC. For Car2Phone this mostly supports the plan to build an AAOS receiver first. It also means the project cannot count on Google Play, because no Indian car shows evidence of Google Automotive Services. Apps reach these cars only through OEM-curated stores, and the cluster can be reached only as a data and theme consumer behind the OEM's safety partition. That is the design Car2Phone already plans.

### Mahindra splits into a Qualcomm-native EV stack and a Visteon/QNX ICE stack

Mahindra is the only Indian OEM whose silicon and OS can both be pinned down. On the INGLO EVs, Mahindra's own release names a **Snapdragon 8295 with 24 GB RAM and 128 GB storage** ([Mahindra](https://auto.mahindra.com/press-release/mahindra-democratises-premium-ev-technology-announces-top-variant-prices-of-be-6-and-xev-9e.html)). Qualcomm describes the platform at over 230,000 DMIPS, driving three 12.3-inch screens, with 60+ apps and OTA ([Qualcomm via Automotive World](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news-releases/qualcomms-snapdragon-digital-chassis-solutions-power-mahindras-first-electric-origin-suv-range-bringing-a-new-era-of-genai-ready-vehicles-to-india/)). Neither release names an OS. The OS evidence comes from elsewhere. Cinemo, selected in June 2025 as Mahindra's infotainment technology provider for these cars, describes "AAOS-based" infotainment with "Android pre-integration" ([Cinemo](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news/cinemo-to-elevate-in-car-entertainment-with-personal-smart-devices-in-mahindras-new-electric-origin-suv-range/)). A November 2026 Mahindra job post asks for HMI work "on Android (AAOS) platforms" for "automotive domain controllers", along with Kanzi, Unreal and Qt skills ([Mahindra Careers](https://jobs.mahindracareers.com/job/Coimbatore-Senior-Engineer-Coim/1382897100/)). No source names a cockpit tier-1 integrator. Component suppliers are named one at a time: **Aptiv** for the optically bonded 12.3-inch screens, **Harman** for audio, and **Yazaki** for the AR-HUD ([Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/feature/revealed-a-to-z-component-suppliers-to-mahindras-latest-bevs-124391)). This points to Mahindra integrating the cockpit itself. Voice moved to a **Google Cloud Gemini Enterprise** agent in August 2026. That is a cloud deal, not a "Google built-in" deal ([Google Cloud](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-08-15-Google-Cloud-and-Mahindra-Partner-to-Redefine-the-In-Car-Experience-with-Gemini-Enterprise)).

The AdrenoX ICE stack is older and better documented. BlackBerry confirmed in November 2021 that the XUV700 cockpit domain controller runs on the **QNX Neutrino RTOS and QNX Hypervisor**, which consolidates "mixed criticality" operating environments on one chip ([PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/mahindra--mahindra-ltd-selects-blackberry-qnx-to-power-cockpit-domain-controller-for-next-generation-xuv700-suv-301427534.html)). Visteon supplies the SmartCore hardware on 3rd-generation Snapdragon cockpit silicon ([MarkLines](https://www.marklines.com/en/news/257326)). Its successor, **SmartCore Pro**, drives the three-screen XUV 7XO ([Visteon](https://www.visteon.com/investors/investor-news/news-details/2026/Visteon-and-Mahindra-Unveil-Next-Generation-SmartCore-Pro-Technology-for-Indias-Best-Selling-SUV/default.aspx)). Mahindra lists that car on an **SA8155P** with 49 apps "powered by Fun&Work" and a BYOD "Theatre mode" ([Mahindra](https://auto.mahindra.com/press-release/mahindra-premieres-xuv-7xo-with-5-pioneering-tech-disruptions.html)). The Android guest version rests on a single unverified forum report of Android 9 on the XUV700. The most likely architecture is a QNX host with a safety-oriented cluster partition and an Android IVI guest, but no vendor has confirmed it.

### Tata buys Harman, curates apps through Ignite, and is moving to a Qualcomm domain controller

Harman says it has worked with Tata for about a decade on "intelligent cockpits and car audio" ([Harman](https://news.harman.com/releases/harman-automotive-and-tata-motors-deepen-decade-long-partnership-with-launch-of-innovative-harman-ignite-platform-in-tata-passenger-vehicles)). Tata's own pages credit Harman for both the 10.25-inch head unit and the **10.25-inch digital cluster** on the Nexon ([Tata](https://cars.tatamotors.com/nexon/ice/edition/camo.html)). Visteon is the exception. It supplied the 2019 Harrier's SmartCore, which ran infotainment and cluster on one SoC "isolated by core virtualisation" ([Visteon](https://www.visteon.com/investors/investor-news/news-details/2019/Visteon-strengthens-cockpit-domain-controller-leadership-with-India-first-SmartCore-program-on-TATA-Harrier-SUV-05-28-2019/default.aspx)), and it supplies today's Punch.ev and Punch Camo 10.25-inch units ([Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/news-international/visteon-launches-30-products-in-q3-including-tata-punch-camo-editions-infotainment-system-123619)). The OS evidence for Tata is indirect but consistent. The **Ignite Store** behind Arcade.ev is "fully compliant with Android Automotive Operating System (AAOS) standards" ([Harman](https://news.harman.com/releases/harman-automotive-and-tata-motors-deepen-decade-long-partnership-with-launch-of-innovative-harman-ignite-platform-in-tata-passenger-vehicles)). The Sierra's Ready Link marketplace is billed as a platform "for in-vehicle Android applications" ([Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/news/harman-technologies-power-tata-sierras-return-with-advanced-connectivity-features-130693)). Tata also hires for an "Embedded Software AAOS" role covering AOSP and vHAL ([job listing](https://mytat.org/job-listing/senior-manager-embedded-software-aaos-407051)). If both of the reports above are correct, Ignite runs on Visteon's Punch.ev unit as well as Harman's hardware. That suggests the store is a head-unit-agnostic AAOS layer.

The Harrier.ev marks Tata's move to a centralised compute. Autocar Pro reports its cockpit domain controller uses "**high-compute Qualcomm Snapdragon chips and Continental processors**" under Tata's T.idal software layer ([Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/feature/harrier-ev-a-window-into-tatas-reimagined-electric-portfolio-126886)). The 14.53-inch Harman screen uses a Samsung Neo QLED panel ([Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/news/harman-launches-samsung-neo-qled-display-for-tata-harrierev-126930)). No part number is public. The Sierra adds a **12.3-inch passenger screen**, which makes it Tata's first three-display car ([Cartoq](https://www.cartoq.com/car-news/tata-sierra-ev-spotted-with-triple-screen-layout-ahead-of-launch/)). One operational detail matters for Car2Phone. The 2025 Altroz already shows **phone Google Maps or Apple Maps in its 10.25-inch cluster** ([Autocar India](https://www.autocarindia.com/car-news/tata-altroz-facelift-interior-features-shared-with-nexon-curvv-435268)). Tata head units therefore already forward projected content into the cluster, though only through closed Android Auto and CarPlay paths.

### Maruti and Toyota run three stacks, and Suzuki chose Linux to avoid lock-in

Maruti specifies and brands its head units in-house as "SmartPlay" and rarely names who builds them. **Bosch** is the only confirmed hardware supplier. Its Android-based, India-built 10.1-inch SmartPlay Pro X debuted on the Victoris in October 2025 ([MarkLines](https://www.marklines.com/en/news/334055)). It comes with an "integrated app store having 35+ apps" and OTA updates ([Maruti](https://www.marutisuzuki.com/corporate/media/press-releases/2025/september/maruti-suzuki-launches-the-all-new-victoris)), and it has now reached the 2026 Brezza. The older Studio, Pro and Pro+ units have no disclosed OS. Maruti's infotainment job ads ask for both **AAOS and Linux** ([Foundit](https://www.foundit.in/job/dm-mgr-infotainment-system-maruti-suzuki-gurugram-18310447)). Harman's role in the older units is strongly hinted but not detailed. Harman publishes the SmartPlay Studio companion app ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.harman.smartlink&hl=en_IN)), and Harman India's MD describes an "almost eight-year" Maruti relationship covering infotainment on 10+ car lines ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/harman-indias-role-in-automotive-technology-revolution-in-conversation-with-md-krishna-kumar-gopalakrishnan-424561-2024-04-06)).

The e Vitara breaks with all of this. Qt and Suzuki confirmed in September 2025 that **both the cluster and the infotainment run Qt on Automotive Grade Linux**. Suzuki's E/E chief said Qt was chosen to "optimize development costs without falling into vendor lock-in" ([Qt/Cision](https://news.cision.com/qt-group/r/suzuki-chooses-qt-to-power-its-digital-cockpit-for-mainstream-evs,c4235341)). Suzuki's "SDV Right" strategy says this approach will spread across the lineup after the e Vitara ([Global Suzuki](https://www.globalsuzuki.com/globalnews/2025/0909.html)). The car is built only in Gujarat, so the India-market version almost certainly shares this stack, though no source says so explicitly. Clusters remain Maruti's weak spot as a display target. The Grand Vitara in 2022 was the **first Maruti with a full-digital cluster** ([CarWale](https://carwale.com/news/maruti-suzuki-grand-vitara-digital-instrument-cluster-revealed)). Most volume models still pair analog dials with a small MID, according to the researchers' trim-level review; no sourced share figure exists. Toyota's India rebadges reuse the donor hardware. The Glanza took the Baleno's SmartPlay unit under the "Smart Playcast" name ([Autocar India](https://www.autocarindia.com/car-news/glanza-to-become-first-toyota-in-india-with-android-auto-apple-carplay-412927)). Toyota's own Arene platform, which launched on the RAV4 with Panasonic IVI, has no announced India deployment ([Toyota UK](https://media.toyota.co.uk/?p=62772)).

### Hyundai, Kia, MG and Skoda-VW disclose services, not silicon

Hyundai and Kia head units most likely come from **Hyundai Mobis**. A Mobis-branded Creta service part is listed on a parts catalogue ([Boodmo](https://boodmo.com/catalog/4697-infotainment_system/m11253-hyundai-creta/)), but Hyundai Motor India's 2024 DRHP does not name an infotainment supplier ([DRHP](https://www.hyundai.com/content/dam/hyundai/in/en/data/investor-relations/HMILDRHP.pdf)). The biggest upcoming change is **Pleos Connect**. It is Hyundai Motor Group's Android Automotive platform, with an app marketplace and a "Pleos Playground" developer environment, and Hyundai targets about 20 million vehicles by 2030 ([Hyundai Motor Group](https://www.hyundaimotorgroup.com/en/news/CONT0000000000172905)). The claim that the next-gen Creta brings it to India rests on spy-shot reporting only ([CarWale](https://www.carwale.com/news/hyundais-pleos-connect-infotainment-system-breaks-cover/)). JSW MG positions **Jio** as its services layer: eSIM, the Hello Jio Hinglish voice assistant ([Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/business/autos/260623/jio-platforms-to-power-in-car-voice-experience-for-mg-motor-india.html)), and an MG App Store built on Jio Store ([Hector Tomahawk brochure](https://s7ap1.scene7.com/is/content/mgmotor/mgmotor/documents/MG%20Hector%20Tomahawk%20PHEV%20-%20Brochure.pdf)). An app store implies an Android runtime. The researchers infer SAIC/SGMW-lineage hardware because the Windsor and Comet are Wuling-derived, but no source confirms it.

## Supplier landscape: Harman and Visteon dominate, Qualcomm owns the premium tier

India's passenger-car cockpit supply base is concentrated. **Harman** (Samsung) is the most pervasive infotainment supplier. Its Pune plant makes infotainment units, speakers and TCUs, and about **4,000 engineers** in Bengaluru and Pune make India its largest engineering base worldwide. Harman expects its India automotive revenue to double in five years ([Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/news/harman-india-expects-automotive-business-to-double-in-five-years-133638)). Its stated Pune capacity target is 1.4 million infotainment units and 800,000 TCUs a year by 2027 ([just-auto](https://www.just-auto.com/news/harman-invest-expand-plant-pune/)). **Visteon** is the incumbent for cockpit domain controllers. It holds every SmartCore program in India (Harrier 2019, XUV700, Scorpio-N, XUV 7XO) as well as cluster wins at Hyundai and Kia, and it describes SmartCore as digital clusters combined with "Android-based infotainment" ([Automotive World](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news/visteon-and-qualcomm-work-together-to-accelerate-development-of-next-generation-digital-cockpit/)). **Bosch** has entered the head-unit business through Maruti's Pro X. **Qualcomm** is the only premium SoC with confirmed Indian design wins: Mahindra INGLO, Mahindra via Visteon, and the Tata Harrier.ev. In February 2026 it signed Tata Electronics to make Snapdragon automotive modules at the planned Assam OSAT plant ([Tata Electronics](https://www.tataelectronics.com/w/qualcomm-and-tata-electronics-partner-to-manufacture-qualcomm-automotive-modules-in-india)). The researchers found **no Indian passenger-car design wins** for MediaTek, TI Jacinto, NXP i.MX or Renesas R-Car, so the entry and mid-segment SoC market is effectively undocumented.

The Indian-owned tier-1s are stronger in two-wheelers than in cars, and that is where the phone-to-display precedent already exists. **Pricol** is India's dominant cluster maker, with an estimated **75–80% of the two-wheeler TFT cluster market**. Its cluster business (DICVS) brought in Rs 2,425 crore, or about 61% of FY26 revenue, and is being demerged into **Pricol Autotech** ([Axis Securities](https://rakesh-jhunjhunwala.in/wp-content/uploads/Pricol-Ltd-Initiating-Coverage-Report-07092026_07-09-2026_07.pdf)). Pricol has shipped Bluetooth "connected clusters" compatible with Android and iOS since about FY2020 ([Pricol AR 2020](https://pricol.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/AnnualReport2020.pdf)). It exited passenger-car clusters by selling that business to Visteon and Denso ([Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/feature/pricol-page-21413)). **UNO Minda** reaches cockpits through its Denso Ten joint ventures, which make infotainment and display audio ([UNO Minda](https://www.unominda.com/investors/interactive-annual-report/strong-partnership.html)). **Minda Corporation** (Spark Minda) announced a Qualcomm Snapdragon Cockpit collaboration for **Android- and QNX-based** software-defined cockpits in July 2025, with no production program named yet ([Construction World](https://www.constructionworld.in/latest-construction-technology/minda--qualcomm-join-forces-for-smart-auto-cockpit-tech/76756)). **Varroc** has begun infotainment deliveries and estimates "true digital cockpit" penetration in India at about **20–25%** ([Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/news/digital-cockpits-ev-electronics-drive-varrocs-next-growth-push-133564)). Among software services firms, **Tata Elxsi** markets an eCockpit with Automotive Android infotainment and a Linux cluster on one SoC ([Tata Elxsi](https://www.tataelxsi.com/industries/automotive/case/ecockpit-services)), and **Sasken** offers Android, AGL and QNX cockpit work including hypervisor guest bring-up ([Sasken](https://www.sasken.com/industries/automotive/integrated-cockpit)). Neither is publicly tied to a production Indian program, and the researchers collected no cockpit facts for KPIT, LTTS or Tata Technologies. Market-size estimates disagree by up to 7x. India infotainment ranges from about USD 0.67B ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/india-automotive-infotainment-market)) to USD 4.5B ([MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/geography/in-car-vehicle-infotainment-ici-systems-market/India)), so neither figure should anchor a business case.

## Nine evidence gaps that block a confident build plan

The largest gap is the **cluster OS**. No current Indian car has a publicly named cluster OS, RTOS, hypervisor guest or rendering engine. The closest data points are the QNX Hypervisor on Mahindra's AdrenoX and Qt on AGL on the e Vitara. Second, **SoCs below the premium tier are unknown**. Every Harman, Bosch, Mobis, Visteon Play and MG unit lacks a named chip, and the researchers found no teardown of any Indian head unit. Third, **Google Automotive Services status** is unconfirmed everywhere. Mahindra is hiring for GAS certification, but no Indian car advertises Google built-in, and Mappls, TomTom and Jio fill the roles GAS would normally take. Fourth, the **Android version and the AAOS vs. custom-Android question** remain open for Tata, Maruti Pro X and MG; "Android-based" in a supplier summary does not settle which one. Fifth, the **head-unit tier-1 for Hyundai, Kia, MG and most Maruti lines** is inferred, not sourced. Sixth, **Pleos Connect's India timing** rests on spy shots. Seventh, Mahindra's **XEV 9S SoC** has conflicting reports (8155 vs. 8295). Eighth, **regulation beyond AIS-071** has not been checked, including any newer display-based tell-tale or driver-distraction rules. Ninth, several key primary sources were never read in full: the Hyundai DRHP, Maruti's FY25–26 annual reports, Team-BHP firmware threads (blocked with HTTP 403), and KPIT and Tata Technologies filings.

Most of these gaps can be closed cheaply. A Mobis Creta head unit, a SmartPlay Pro+ unit and a Harman 10.25-inch Tata unit can all be bought as service parts for a bench teardown that identifies the SoC and boot logs. Owner screenshots of Settings/About screens and developer-options menus would settle the Android version per model. Wireless certification filings (WPC, BIS) and OEM job ads, which already exposed AAOS at Mahindra, Tata and Maruti, are the cheapest remaining signals.

## What this means for Car2Phone's India-first plan

**The AAOS-first receiver matches where the volume is heading, but distribution will go through OEM stores, not Google Play.** Mahindra INGLO, Tata's Ignite-equipped cars, Maruti's Pro X, MG's Jio Store cars and Hyundai/Kia's future Pleos cars all expose an Android app runtime. Not one of them shows evidence of Google Play. The car-side receiver should therefore ship with **no Google Mobile Services dependency**, as a self-contained APK that runs on plain AAOS or AOSP. Each OEM store is a separate distribution route with a named gatekeeper. Harman runs a developer program that tests, validates and handles payments for Ignite Store apps ([Harman Ignite developers](https://ignitedevelopers.harman.com/ignite-store-intro); [Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/news/tata-motors-cars-to-offer-harman-ignite-store-inside-in-car-infotainment-systems-120275)). Pleos has Playground, and Maruti and MG curate their own catalogues. Harman Ignite is the highest-leverage first partner, because one listing could reach most Tata models and possibly Visteon-built units too. Mahindra INGLO is the best technical showcase: an SA8295P driving **three 12.3-inch screens including a passenger display**, with Wi-Fi 6 on board ([Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/feature/revealed-a-to-z-component-suppliers-to-mahindras-latest-bevs-124391)). Car2Phone should treat **Cinemo**, which already lets passengers' phones share media and video across Mahindra's screens, as both the closest competitor and a possible integration partner ([Cinemo](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news/cinemo-to-elevate-in-car-entertainment-with-personal-smart-devices-in-mahindras-new-electric-origin-suv-range/)).

**The portable Linux/QNX core has two concrete Indian targets.** The first is **AGL with Qt**, on the e Vitara and, if Suzuki's "SDV Right" plan holds, across Maruti's next generation. The second is the **QNX Hypervisor host** on Mahindra's Visteon controllers. This makes the core's language and dependency choices decisive. It should be a C/C++ library with no Android assumptions, a Qt/QML binding for the cluster-theme renderer on AGL, and a build that works on QNX. Suzuki explicitly chose Qt to avoid vendor lock-in, so an open, Qt-native cluster-theme component fits Maruti's stated philosophy better than an Android-only one.

**The decision to render safety items locally is the only viable cluster model, and India's market structure confirms it.** AIS-071 requires tell-tales to follow its rules on symbols, colour and visibility ([ARAI AIS-071](https://hmr.araiindia.com/Control/AIS/330201931818PMAIS-071_Part_1_Rev_1.pdf)). The documented Indian architectures put the cluster in a separate, more trusted domain: QNX's "mixed criticality" partitioning, Visteon's virtualised single-SoC design, and Renesas's split between a real-time tell-tale domain and an Android domain ([Renesas](https://www.renesas.com/en/blogs/designing-digital-dashboards-e-mobility-tft-instrument-clusters-e-scooters-bikes-and-urban-evs)). A Car2Phone cluster client therefore needs to be a **guest inside the OEM's cluster HMI**, consuming themes and a narrow data schema (navigation cards, media metadata, colours) through an OEM-approved channel. It should not be a video sink. Tata's Altroz already shows phone maps in the cluster through Android Auto and CarPlay, which proves OEMs will expose a cluster content zone. Car2Phone's job is to make that zone open rather than Google- or Apple-only. The **addressable cluster base is small**: full-TFT clusters are concentrated in Mahindra, Tata, Kia and premium Maruti trims, so the passenger and center screens carry most of the near-term value.

**India's two-wheeler market is an underrated beachhead for the data and theme protocol.** Pricol, Bosch, NXP and Renesas already ship or reference **Bluetooth phone-to-cluster pairing** at mass-market scale ([Pricol](https://pricol.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/AnnualReport2020.pdf); [NXP](https://www.nxp.com/company/about-nxp/smarter-world-blog/BL-NXP-AND-TESSOLVE)), but each OEM uses its own proprietary app. Car2Phone's cluster data channel is the part that does not need video bandwidth. If it is specified as transport-agnostic (Wi-Fi and BLE), it could become the open alternative for 2W TFT clusters, and Pricol Autotech is the obvious partner. This is an inference from the precedent; no 2W OEM has signalled interest.

**The priority order follows from the evidence.** Mahindra INGLO and the XUV 7XO come first, because they have documented Qualcomm, AAOS and QNX stacks and passenger screens. Tata through Harman Ignite comes second, for the largest single-store reach. Maruti Pro X and the e Vitara come third, because they cover both receiver ports. Hyundai and Kia follow once Pleos lands in India. MG and Skoda-VW come last, because nothing below their service layers is public. Until a partnership exists, wireless Android Auto and CarPlay, which every brand ships, remain the only universal way onto the center screen. Car2Phone's early messaging should present the project as complementary to them (more screens, open cluster themes), not as a replacement.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question Car2Phone faces in India. Whether Indian cars run Android is largely settled: their center screens are Android-based, and the new flagships are moving to AAOS. The real constraint is who controls the app channel and the cluster partition. Every Indian OEM keeps both closed. App catalogues are curated by Harman, Jio or the OEM, and clusters sit behind QNX, hypervisor or Qt-on-Linux boundaries that no public source has documented. Car2Phone's technical design (an AAOS receiver, a portable core, and local rendering of safety items) is sound. Its critical path is commercial: one store listing (Harman Ignite) and one OEM cluster-theme integration (Mahindra or Maruti's Qt stack) would prove the model more than any amount of engineering.

The second insight is that the market's openness runs in the opposite direction to its sophistication. Mahindra's most capable cockpit is integrated in-house and already ships a phone-to-passenger-screen feature through Cinemo, so a proprietary competitor holds the best target. Suzuki, the most cost-conscious OEM, chose Qt on Linux specifically to avoid vendor lock-in. An open-source project fits that rationale better than any tier-1 product does. Car2Phone should target Suzuki's AGL/Qt stack early with its portable core, not treat it as a later-phase port.
